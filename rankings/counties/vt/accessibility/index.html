What each check means

  • Links have a discernible name. Links have unique, descriptive text so users know where they go. Screen reader users cannot identify or interact with elements.
  • [aria-*] attributes match their roles. Accessibility attributes are only used where they are supported. Screen readers ignore attributes that don't match the element's role.
  • Document has a main landmark. The page has exactly one main content area so screen readers can navigate to it. Screen reader users cannot skip directly to main content.
  • Heading elements appear in a sequentially-descending order. Page headings follow a logical order without skipping levels. Keyboard users cannot efficiently navigate your site.
  • Skip links are focusable. Skip links are visible and reachable by keyboard so users can bypass navigation. Screen reader users cannot identify or interact with elements.

About accessibility

Meet WCAG standards so every type of user can use your website.

Why it's important

People with disabilities need equal access to information and services online.

Risks

  • People with disabilities cannot use your site.

Guidance

About this data

ScanGov scans the homepage of each county government site on our county list in Vermont. Scores come from the checks listed on standards.scangov.org. Sites were scanned between 9/16/2026 and 9/16/2026; each site's report shows its own scan date. A site counts as fully scanned when its homepage returned successfully and sent the scanner its page content. Averages, grade shares, failure rates, and ranks use fully scanned sites only. Grades follow the ScanGov grade scale, and rank follows the ScanGov ranking method. The full dataset, including every county's raw results, is published in the ScanGov data repository.
