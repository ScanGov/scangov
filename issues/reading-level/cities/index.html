City websites that are hard to read

50 of 199 fully scanned U.S. city websites read above an 8th grade level. The share in each state and every site listed, from ScanGov homepage scans.

  • Sites: 266
  • Last scan: 11/7/2026
  • All
  • Cities

Of the 199 fully scanned U.S. city sites we tested, 50 (25%) read above an 8th grade level. Hard text slows people down. Some give up before they find what they need. We score the text on each homepage with the Automated Readability Index (ARI). It estimates the U.S. school grade a reader needs. Another 13 had too little text on the homepage to score. They get no pass or fail. Among states with at least 10 tested sites, Texas has the highest share (30%). California has the lowest (20%). This page uses the Reading level check. It passes when the homepage text reads at or below an 8th grade level. That means an ARI score of 8.5 or lower. We need at least 100 words to score a page.

About this check

Reading level is one of the readability checks. The homepage text reads at or below an 8th grade level.

Why it matters

Plain text helps people get what they need the first time. Hard text leads to more calls, more errors, and more visits.

Risk

Users struggle to understand your content.

Guidance

  • Reading level standard, with how to fix it
  • 21st Century Integrated Digital Experience Act (IDEA)
  • Automated Readability Index (ARI)
  • Flesch–Kincaid readability tests
  • U.S. Federal Website Standards
  • Plain Writing Act of 2010
  • City readability rankings

About this data

ScanGov scans the homepage of each county site on our county list. Scores come from the checks in the ScanGov standards. Sites were scanned between 9/30/2026 and 11/5/2026. Each site's report shows its own scan date. A site counts as fully scanned when its homepage loaded and sent us its page content. Averages, grade shares, failure rates, and ranks use fully scanned sites only. We could not fully scan 54 sites. They did not respond, blocked the scanner, or sent no page content. They appear in the table without grades, with their scan status, and are left out of every figure. Grades follow the ScanGov grade scale. Rank follows the ScanGov ranking method. The full dataset, with every county's raw results, is in the ScanGov data repository.

13 fully scanned city sites had too little text on the homepage to score. They are left out of the share.
