County websites that are hard to read

899 of 2,320 fully scanned U.S. county government websites read above an 8th grade level. A map, the share in each state, and every site listed, from ScanGov homepage scans.

  • Sites: 2832
  • Last scan: 10/26/2026

Of the 2,320 fully scanned U.S. county sites we tested, 899 (39%) read above an 8th grade level. Hard text slows people down. Some give up before they find what they need. We score the text on each homepage with the Automated Readability Index (ARI). It estimates the U.S. school grade a reader needs. Among states with at least 10 tested sites, Maryland has the highest share (67%). Nebraska has the lowest (18%). This page uses the Reading level check. It passes when page text reads at or below a 7th grade level so most users can understand it. A site passes when its ARI score is 8.5 or lower.

About this check

Reading level is one of the readability checks. Page text reads at or below a 7th grade level so most users can understand it.

Why it matters

Plain text helps people get what they need the first time. Hard text leads to more calls, more errors, and more visits.

Risk

Users struggle to understand your content.

Guidance

  • Reading level standard, with how to fix it
  • 21st Century Integrated Digital Experience Act (IDEA)
  • Automated Readability Index (ARI)
  • Flesch–Kincaid readability tests
  • U.S. Federal Website Standards
  • Plain Writing Act of 2010
  • Counties readability rankings

About this data

ScanGov scans the homepage of each county government site on our county list. Scores come from the checks in the ScanGov standards. Sites were scanned between 7/18/2026 and 10/25/2026. Each site's report shows its own scan date. A site counts as fully scanned when its homepage loaded and sent us its page content. Averages, grade shares, failure rates, and ranks use fully scanned sites only. We could not fully scan 401 sites. They did not respond, blocked the scanner, or sent no page content. They appear in the table without grades, with their scan status, and are left out of every figure. Grades follow the ScanGov grade scale. Rank follows the ScanGov ranking method. The full dataset, with every county's raw results, is in the ScanGov data repository.

111 fully scanned county sites have no result for this check and are left out of the share.
