About these checks

A robots.txt file tells search engines and AI agents which parts of a site they may crawl and where to find the sitemap. When a site has one, it has to follow the format and not block the whole site. These botability checks test each part of that. A site with no robots.txt file passes both checks, since nothing blocks crawling and there is no file to get wrong. Sites whose robots.txt could not be fetched are left out of the share.

  • Robots valid. The site's robots.txt file is written correctly so search engines can follow it. Search engines ignore crawling rules they cannot read.
  • Robots allowed. The robots.txt file allows search engines and AI agents to crawl the site. Your site is blocked from search results.

About this data

ScanGov scans the homepage of each county site on our county list. Scores come from the checks in the ScanGov standards. Sites were scanned between 9/30/2026 and 11/8/2026. Each site's report shows its own scan date. A site counts as fully scanned when its homepage loaded and sent us its page content. Averages, grade shares, failure rates, and ranks use fully scanned sites only. We could not fully scan 304 sites. They did not respond, blocked the scanner, or sent no page content. They appear in the table without grades, with their scan status, and are left out of every figure. Grades follow the ScanGov grade scale. Rank follows the ScanGov ranking method. The full dataset, with every county's raw results, is in the ScanGov data repository.
