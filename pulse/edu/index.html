What we scan

ScanGov scans the homepage of every public college and university site on our list, 816 sites. Each scan runs the checks on standards.scangov.org for accessibility, botability, security, usability, and readability. A site counts as fully scanned when its homepage responded and returned page content; sites that did not are listed with their scan status and left out of every figure here. How sites are doing

Of 705 fully scanned sites, 23% (164) earn an A for overall score, 75% (531) a B or C, and 1% (10) a D or F. The average score is 84. The curve shows where scores cluster; the bar shows the share in each grade band.

Where sites fail

For each indicator, the five checks that fully scanned education sites fail most often. A site can fail more than one check, and each check links to its standard and how to fix it.

Accessibility

Meet WCAG standards so every type of user can use your website. The most common accessibility failure is Heading elements appear in a sequentially-descending order: 22% (158 of 705 sites) fail it.

What each accessibility check means
  • Heading elements appear in a sequentially-descending order. Page headings follow a logical order without skipping levels. Keyboard users cannot efficiently navigate your site.
  • Touch targets have sufficient size and spacing. Buttons and interactive elements are large enough to tap easily on touch screens. Users with motor disabilities struggle to tap small buttons.
  • Links have a discernible name. Links have unique, descriptive text so users know where they go. Screen reader users cannot identify or interact with elements.
  • Elements with visible text labels have matching accessible names. A button or link's visible label matches what screen readers announce. Voice control users cannot activate elements by their visible label.
  • Background and foreground colors have a sufficient contrast ratio. Text and background colors meet minimum contrast ratios. Users with low vision cannot read your text.

Botability

Help AI and search engines discover, crawl, and index your website. The most common botability failure is Sitemap in robots.txt: 38% (268 of 705 sites) fail it.

What each botability check means
  • Sitemap in robots.txt. The robots.txt file links to the sitemap. Search engines take longer to find your pages.
  • Link text. Links use descriptive text that tells users and search engines where they go. Users cannot tell where links go.
  • Sitemap status. The sitemap file loads and returns a successful response. Search engines cannot find all your pages.
  • Sitemap XML. The sitemap file uses the required XML format. Search engines cannot read your sitemap.
  • Robots valid. The site's robots.txt file is written correctly so search engines can follow it. Search engines ignore crawling rules they cannot read.
Botability: Standards · Guidance · Project ScanGov rankings · How ScanGov helps

Security

Secure your site and protect users with proper security practices. The most common security failure is X-Content-Type-Options: 56% (393 of 705 sites) fail it.

What each security check means
  • X-Content-Type-Options. The site tells browsers not to guess the type of files it serves. Browsers might run dangerous files by mistake.
  • Errors in the console. The page loads without JavaScript or network errors. Site may have hidden bugs that break features.
  • Security.txt. The site has a security.txt file so researchers know how to report issues. Security researchers cannot report problems to you.
  • Sponsored top-level domain (sTLD). The site uses a government-issued domain (.gov, .edu, or .mil). People cannot tell if your site is real government.
Security: Standards · Guidance · Project ScanGov rankings · How ScanGov helps

Usability

Optimize content structure so users quickly find what they need. The most common usability failure is Open Graph image alt: 88% (618 of 705 sites) fail it.

What each usability check means
  • Open Graph image alt. The social media preview image has alt text for screen readers. Screen readers cannot describe shared images.
  • Largest Contentful Paint (LCP). The page's main content loads within 2.5 seconds. Users wait too long and leave your page.
  • First Contentful Paint (FCP). The page starts displaying content within 1.8 seconds. Users see blank screen and think site is broken.
  • Open Graph site name. The site has a name for social media and browser display. Site name missing in social media shares.
  • Open Graph image. The page has a preview image for social media cards. Shared links have no image on social media.
Usability: Standards · Guidance · Project ScanGov rankings · How ScanGov helps

Readability

Write content at a reading level most users can understand. The most common readability failure is Reading level: 36% (252 of 705 sites) fail it.

What each readability check means
  • Reading level. Page text reads at or below a 7th grade level so most users can understand it. Users struggle to understand your content.

About this data

ScanGov scans the homepage of every public college and university site on our list. Scores come from the checks in the ScanGov standards. Sites were scanned between 9/23/2026 and 11/1/2026. Each site's report shows its own scan date. A site counts as fully scanned when its homepage loaded and sent us its page content. Averages, grade shares, failure rates, and ranks use fully scanned sites only. We could not fully scan 111 sites. They did not respond, blocked the scanner, or sent no page content. They appear in the table without grades, with their scan status, and are left out of every figure. Grades follow the ScanGov grade scale. Rank follows the ScanGov ranking method. The full dataset, with every county's raw results, is in the ScanGov data repository.
